About The Owner

Gavin Benton was born and raised in North Carolina in 1998. From a young age, he was exposed to dogs and many other animals, as his family curated the Norfolk Zoo. In addition, his family owned a significant amount of land where they bred, trained, and sold purebred wolves, cougars, and sled dogs. As a result, Gavin’s passion for animals—and his understanding of them—grew alongside him. Having spent his entire life around high-drive, pack-oriented, predatory animals, Gavin never developed fear or discomfort around them. Instead, he cultivated a deep level of compassion and an intuitive understanding of animal behavior—much of which cannot be taught in a classroom. This lifelong exposure allowed him to develop a natural ability to read, communicate with, and manage complex animal behavior. Gavin never stopped pursuing his love for animals and has continued to expand his knowledge and hands-on experience throughout his life. In 2019, he decided to pursue a career in law enforcement while continuing to work with dogs during his off-duty hours. During this time, he further developed his skills by helping friends, families, and animal shelters address behavioral challenges in dogs. Today, while still working full time as a police officer, Gavin is the owner and operator of North Port K9 Academy, based in Southwest Florida. He specializes in behavior modification for large-breed, working-line dogs, focusing on issues such as fear, reactivity, aggression, and anxiety. His approach emphasizes structure, clarity, confidence-building, and effective communication between dog and handler. Recently, Gavin launched a GoFundMe initiative and began using his knowledge, skills, and resources to take in dogs facing behavioral surrender or euthanasia. He rehabilitates and rehomes these dogs free of charge, with the goal of preventing them from entering shelters or being unnecessarily put down. In his remaining free time, Gavin travels to various clinics, teaching classes on animal behavior and educating handlers on proper training, rehabilitation, and responsible ownership. Gavin operates by a simple but powerful philosophy: “Dogs aren’t bad—they’re just misunderstood.”
